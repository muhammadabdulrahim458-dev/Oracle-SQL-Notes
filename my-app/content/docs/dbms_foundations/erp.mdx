## Enterprise Applications and ERP Systems

### 1. Big Picture
Large organisations use integrated software suites called ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) to manage all aspects of business – finance, HR, supply chain, etc. These systems rely heavily on databases to unify data across departments.

### 2. Simple Explanation
ERP is like the **central nervous system** of a company. It connects departments so that everyone uses the same data. For example, when sales orders a product, inventory automatically updates, and accounting records the revenue – all because they share a common database.

### 3. Key Terms
- **ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning)**: Integrated software that manages core business processes.
- **Modules**: Separate functional areas (e.g., Finance, HR, Manufacturing).
- **CRM (Customer Relationship Management)**: Manages customer interactions – often integrated with ERP.
- **SCM (Supply Chain Management)**: Manages logistics and suppliers.

### 4. Real World Example
- **SAP**, **Oracle ERP**, **Microsoft Dynamics** – these are popular ERP systems.
- **University**: Not typical, but a university management system similar to ERP (student records, finance, HR) – all share a database.
- **E‑commerce**: Order management, inventory, customer data all in one ERP – but often they use custom microservices instead.

### 5. Software Engineer Perspective
We may work on integrating ERP systems with other applications using APIs, or building custom modules. Understanding the data model of an ERP is crucial for reporting and analytics. In modern companies, we often replace monolithic ERP with microservices, but the need for a unified database remains.

### 6. Exam Perspective
- **Definition** of ERP.
- **Role** of database in ERP – centralised storage, integration.
- **Benefits** of ERP: streamlined processes, real‑time data, better decision‑making.
- **Challenges**: cost, complexity, customisation.

### 7. Common Mistakes
- Thinking ERP is just a product – it's a philosophy of integration.
- Forgetting that ERP relies on a central database – that's the key connection to this course.

### 8. Quick Revision Box
- ERP = integrated business software.
- Uses a shared database for all modules.
- Provides consistent, real‑time data across departments.
- Examples: SAP, Oracle ERP, Microsoft Dynamics.
